## September 6: totals versus public market

Source: [market-comparison artifact JSON](../experimental_daily_totals/2026-09-06/market-comparison-20260906T194325Z/artifact.json). Its inputs are the [comparison query](../experimental_daily_totals/2026-09-06/market-comparison-20260906T194325Z/comparison.sql), the [market-observation metadata](../experimental_daily_totals/2026-09-06/market-observation-20260906T194325Z/metadata.json), and the [September 6 totals model output](../experimental_daily_totals/2026-09-06/20260906T192716990269Z-8c8a3aedec1403e7.json).

The artifact compares the internal model with saved public DraftKings totals prices for four games. All four are PASS. ATH–SEA has the largest positive gap between projected total and market line, but that descriptive difference is not a validated hit probability, confidence signal, or expected return. A higher model mean does not by itself imply that over outcomes occur more often; prices and the residual-sample distribution also matter. The chart displays gap size only.

The support table uses 769 development residuals. Over/under shares are empirical sample frequencies, not calibrated probabilities. For integer totals, pushes are shown separately; the conditional over/under shares exclude pushes. Break-even rates are arithmetic conversions from American odds (for integer lines, conditional on a non-push outcome), not model probabilities. The artifact notes that NYY–SD and MIN–CWS sample shares do not reach either side's break-even rate. Higher-looking over support for ATH–SEA or under support for WSH–LAD is not promoted to a recommendation because probability calibration and price validity are unverified.

The model uses team runs and venue history through September 5; it does not account for starting pitchers, lineups, or weather. A separate 417-game final-total MAE of 3.47 runs is a diagnostic, not betting validation. The DraftKings page was observed in New York at 3:43 p.m. on September 6; provider update time and executable price were unknown. This is a research observation, not formally admitted bookmaker input, and must not be reused as pregame evidence after a game starts. The artifact's next-step notes call for connecting timestamped one-book prices and full schedules to an evaluator, validating residual shares on a separate chronological sample, and accumulating future pregame observations against official results without backfilling historical prices.

## September 24: response-source cards

Source: [September 24 response-sources JSON](2026-09-24/response-sources.json). These cards cover both the September 23 result diagnostic and the September 24 model/market response; their populations should not be conflated.

For September 23, the all-game diagnostic compares 16 games, while the formal settlement covers 15. The first TOR–BAL game was not included in formal settlement because the official response contained both postponement and completion dates and the existing collector rejected it. “Win accuracy” means the share of games where the initially saved pregame model-selected team matched the official winner; it is not betting ROI. Brier score is the mean squared error between home-win probability and the actual home-win indicator; lower is better. A single 16-game day cannot establish long-run probability reliability. The source card timestamps the New York game date as 2026-09-23 and result collection as 2026-09-24 20:15:59–20:16:30 UTC.

The separate September 23 totals diagnostic uses only the first valid pregame totals report and official final scores. MAE is the mean absolute difference between projected and actual game totals. It is not an official O/U settlement. The card says not to select a better-looking forecast from eight later reports; an ML ledger value of `totals_status NOT_EVALUATED` also does not mean that no separate totals forecast existed. Its recorded context is a September 22 historical-data cutoff, with the first totals report at 09:14:45 UTC before all games started.

For September 24, “model win probability” is a research-model value from inputs such as starting pitching, lineup, and bullpen, not an independently validated observed hit frequency. “Input completeness” is the model's data-sufficiency score, not a win probability or lineup-publication rate. “Nominal odds difference” is model win probability minus the public price's break-even probability; it is not a validated betting edge. The seven remaining pregame games are covered, including `SCORED_NOT_TOP3`; ML probabilities were produced for only four lineup-ready games, while three West Coast games had no probability. The four scored games had 44.0–61.3% input completeness. Defense and environment inputs were absent; the totals model omits lineup, starting-pitcher, and weather inputs, so a projected-total/market-line gap is not an O/U probability. Public prices were observed on one FanDuel page; account-level executability and the book's update time were unknown. TB–NYY was first captured on the 16:33 EDT recheck; MIL–PHI and CLE–BOS were unchanged then. The CIN–ATL recheck returned HTTP 403, so the earlier 16:15 EDT observation was retained, not represented as a new quote. This was not a formally admitted market trace. Five already-started games were excluded from new pregame predictions.

Definitions repeated across these cards: sample size is the count of eligible games; hits are exact selected-team matches to official winners; accuracy is hits divided by eligible games; MAE is mean absolute total-run error; RMSE is root mean squared total-run error. A public price's break-even rate is the win probability that yields zero expected profit at those odds before any other execution effects; it is not the model's estimated probability.

## Scope and exclusions

This index covers the five presentation families above: the September 6 totals-market artifact; September 13 preparation; September 24 response-source cards; September 29 delivery cards; and September 29–30 operations-source/status snapshots and archived rendering. It translates the principal narrative, definitions, and material caveats, not every repeated field label or all machine data. Source JSON and HTML were not modified.

I did not repeat full reports where an English Markdown companion already exists, including prospective-ML settlement and no-pregame-prediction reviews. I also did not duplicate the standalone readiness or error-notebook translations, nor translate generic schema labels that do not carry explanatory prose. Those source families remain available in their original files.
